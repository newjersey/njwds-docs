## General guidance

### Use sentence case 

Capitalization conventions vary by language. While English defaults to sentence case for headings and interface elements, Spanish and other languages follow different grammatical rules. Spanish headings, for example, typically use title cases. Do not apply English capitalization rules to translated content. Refer to the [NJ Translation and Localization Guide](https://docs.google.com/document/d/12bUhW4SdpTCflhQP-KeRFAy2lNXuP5KfJU7ZwoGRVNg/edit?disco=AAACDm6v9PI&tab=t.tps2rfoz4x3c) for guidance.

When writing English copy, capitalize only the first word and proper nouns for all of the following:
- Page titles and headings (H1–H6)
- Subheadings
- Buttons and calls to action
- Alerts and banners
- Labels, tabs, and system navigation
- Form field labels

This is consistent with [VA.gov](http://VA.gov), [GSA.gov](http://GSA.gov), the [U.S. Web Design System (USWDS)](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/list/), and many other federal institutions. [Research by the USAGov content team](https://www.usa.gov/blog/2023/09/making-the-case-for-sentence-case) found sentence case easier for content designers to apply consistently, better suited to mobile displays (reduces line wrapping), and aligned with modern conversational government style. All without any measurable loss of user trust.

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| Your timeline for action | Your Timeline for Action |
| Save and continue | Save and Continue |
| Apply now for GI Bill benefits | Apply Now For GI Bill Benefits |

### Always capitalize 

- Proper nouns: names of people, places, agencies, products, days of the week and months, and titles of books
- Formal titles of forms: Form 1040, U.S. Individual Income Tax Return.
- Personal titles when they precede a name: “Director López got approval”
- The first word of any sentence, bullet point, or heading
- The first word after a colon only if it begins a complete sentence
- NJ departments and agencies: follow naming and capitalization at [NJ.gov's Department and Agency page](http://NJ.gov), including whether they use "and" or "&"

### Capitalizing "State" 

#### Do not capitalize “state” unless it’s part of a proper name. Keep “state” lowercase if it describes a type of object, tier, or setting (state laws, state troopers, state funded, etc.)

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t | ❓ Why |
| ----------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| DOL administered the survey at the state level | DOL administered the survey at the State level | In this sentence, "state" is a common descriptor modifying the noun "level." It specifies the geographic or administrative scale of the survey (as opposed to the national level or local level). Because it refers to a generic tier of government and does not name a specific state, it is a common adjective. |
| Funding cutbacks have heavily impacted state universities | Funding cutbacks have heavily impacted State universities | The term "state universities" is a generic classification for a type of institution (universities funded by public, state money). You only capitalize "State" here if it is part of the official, legal name of a specific institution. For instance, Ohio State University or San Diego State University. When you are talking about state universities as a broad group, "state" is just a generic descriptor and must stay lowercase. |
| The state of New Jersey | The State of New Jersey | In this example, “state” is a common noun describing the type of entity, while “New Jersey” is the proper noun. 
You wouldn’t capitalize “the city of Chicago” or “the continent of Africa” because the generic descriptor doesn't become part of the official name. |

#### Capitalize "State" when it’s a proper noun, official name, or entity:
- As part of the full name (State of New Jersey)
- When the word it modifies is capitalized (the State Commissioner)
- When referring to the state as an actor (the State filed; doing business with the State)
- When it’s part of a proper name (I loved visiting New Jersey State)

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t | ❓ Why |
| ----------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| The funding was appropriated directly by the State of New Jersey | The funding was appropriated by the state of New Jersey | Capitalizing "State" here indicates you are referring to the specific legislative and governing entity that holds the legal authority to appropriate public funds. |
| The guidelines were approved by the State Commissioner of Education | The guidelines were approved by the state Commissioner of Education | Always capitalize official titles when they serve as a formal name or part of an official department heading. If you wrote, "She is a state commissioner," it would be lowercase because it is a generic description of a job role. |
| The defense argued that the State filed the lawsuit without sufficient evidence. | The defense argued that the state filed the lawsuit without sufficient evidence. | In legal documents, academic case reviews, and court-related context, "the State" replaces the specific name of a person or entity acting in a governmental capacity. Because it functions as a proper name for a specific party in a lawsuit, it must be capitalized. |
| While I have toured New York City, I spent most of my summer visiting New Jersey State. | While I have toured New York City, I spent most of my summer visiting New Jersey state. | "State" is used here as an essential part of a proper geographic name to distinguish it from a city or regional counterpart. |

### Capitalization exceptions 
We use sentence case capitalization as the standard across our products, but there are a few exceptions:
- "Veteran" and "Veterans" ([per federal standards](https://design.va.gov/content-style-guide/capitalization#:~:text=Proper%20nouns%20(such%20as%20branded,and%20Find%20a%20VA%20Location))) 

### Do not capitalize
- Generic references to forms or processes: income taxes, income tax forms
- Words like agile, open source, federal, government, unless it’s the first word of a sentence
- Acronyms or initialisms when spelled out, unless they are a proper noun ("x-ray,” for example, is always lowercase)
- Email addresses and URLs. These are always lowercase: myemail@nj.gov
- Licenses and permits in body copy unless part of an official title: mercantile license vs. Mercantile License Fee Survey

### Avoid all caps
Avoid all caps for any purpose in web content. Longer uppercase text reduces legibility and is harder to read for people with cognitive disabilities or low vision. All caps should only appear when rendered automatically by a component (such as certain button styles). Authors should still enter text in sentence case and let styling handle the rendering.
