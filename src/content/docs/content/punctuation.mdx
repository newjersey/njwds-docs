## Oxford comma
Always use the Oxford (serial) comma in lists of three or more items.

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| I need to bring my ID, proof of address, and Social Security number. | I need to bring my ID, proof of address and Social Security number. |

## Periods

### General guidance

Use a period:
- At the end of descriptions, sentences, and paragraphs
- At the end of error messages and microcopy

Do not use a period:
- After headings and subheadings
- After buttons and CTAs
- If a sentence ends with a website or email address
- Periods in bulleted lists

### Periods in bulleted lists

**Do not use periods for sentence fragments:**

To apply, you will need:
- Your Social Security number
- Proof of New Jersey residency
- Recent tax returns

**Use periods for complete sentences, or any item has 2 or more sentences:**
- Please review your secure member portal for your final decision.
- You will also receive a formal letter in the mail within 10 business days.

**Use a period for all bullets if one item has 2 sentence:**

Here's what you'll need to apply:
- A Social Security number for you, your spouse, and your qualified dependents.
- Your military discharge information. If you sign in to apply, we may be able to fill in this information for you.


## Colons
Use a colon to introduce a list or an explanation. Capitalize the first word after a colon only if it begins a new and complete sentence. 

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| I have several favorite foods: apples, bananas, and chips. | I have several favorite foods: Apples were my first favorite snack, but chips are a rising star. |
| Keep this in mind: You must submit all documents before the deadline. | Keep this in mind: you must submit all documents before the deadline. |

If you’re having trouble deciding: 
- If what follows the colon could stand alone as a sentence, capitalize it
- If it's a list, a phrase, or a fragment that completes the thought from before the colon, keep it lowercase.

## Em dash and hyphens
- Use an em dash (—) to signal an abrupt change or set off a phrase, with [spaces before and after the em dash](https://www.w3.org/guide/manual-of-style/#Punctuation)
- Use a hyphen (-) to join [compound modifiers](https://www.grammarly.com/blog/punctuation-capitalization/hyphen-with-compound-modifiers/) and in phone numbers. No spaces on either side: “The university relies heavily on state-funded grants"
- Do not hyphenate if the compound comes after the noun: “Health regulations are evaluated at the state level”
- Do not use an em dash or hyphen for a range of numbers. Instead, spell it out (“15 to 20”)

## Quotation marks
- The period at the end of a sentence goes inside the quotation marks: We use phrases like "people who live in New Jersey."
- Use curly (smart) quotation marks and apostrophes, not straight ones.
- Use single quotes only when quoting within a quote.

## Ampersand
- Spell out "and" in all body copy and headings
- Use "&" only when it appears in an official agency name: Department of Labor & Workforce Development.

## Slash
- Avoid the slash (/). Use words or commas instead
- If you must use one, no space on either side

## Semicolons
Semicolons may be used to separate information more than a comma conveys but less than a period, to clarify a series, or to link independent clauses. Do not use semicolons at the end of bullet list items.

## Spacing
Use a single space between sentences and after all punctuation. Never use two spaces.
