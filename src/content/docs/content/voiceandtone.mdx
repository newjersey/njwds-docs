We write empowering content by being mindful of our voice and our tone. They're related but different, and understanding the distinction makes every writing decision easier. You have the same voice all the time, but your tone changes based on context. 

You might speak one way with a close friend and another way in a professional setting. You probably wouldn't talk to someone who's anxious or confused the same way you'd talk to someone who just got great news. 

The same is true for us. Our voice is consistent; it reflects who we are as an organization. Our tone adapts to the situation, the context, and the emotional state of the person we're writing for.

## Voice
We work at the intersection of government and people's real lives. We know that navigating benefits, licenses, and public services can feel overwhelming. It’s often full of jargon, dead ends, and forms that seem designed for insiders. We're here to change that.

We write like the knowledgeable neighbor everyone deserves: someone who understands the system, explains it clearly, and wants to help. We take every person's situation seriously. We don't talk down to people, and we don't hide behind institutional language.

From a page heading to an error message, every word we publish should leave someone feeling more capable, not less. When we write, our voice is:

- **Plain-spoken.** Use simple, straightforward language. Get to the point quickly. Avoid jargon, acronyms, and bureaucratic phrasing.
- **Helpful and guiding.** Anticipate the next question before it's asked. Instead of just stating a rule, we explain what it means and what comes next. We move people forward, not just inform them.
- **Direct and honest.** State facts and give real timelines. Say "you must" when something is required and "you can" when something is optional. Don't soften the truth in ways that mislead, and don't overclaim what we can deliver.
- **Trustworthy and credible.** Use accurate information from verified, official sources. Cite guidelines when needed. Avoid words like maybe or hopefully: if a rule exists, state it as a fact.
- **Empathetic and respectful.** Acknowledge that government processes can be challenging. Use supportive language. Never blame or talk down to users.
- **Empowering.** Help users feel in control. Teach, don't just tell. Use "you" and "your" to clarify ownership of tasks, and "we" and "our" to humanize the agency.
- **Inclusive.** New Jersey's residents speak many languages, come from many backgrounds, and have many levels of digital experience. [We write inclusive, people-centered content for all of them](https://grove.nj.gov/content/inclusive/). We don't assume prior knowledge, and we don't make people feel like outsiders in their own government.


## Tone

Our tone is clear, respectful, and adaptable. Before you write, consider the emotional state of the person you're writing for. Are they starting a new application, hopeful and ready? Are they confused and looking for help? Have they just hit an error that's blocking them from something important?

| Context | Tone approach | Example |
| ----------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| Introduction | Friendly, encouraging | We'll guide you through applying for benefits. It takes about 20 minutes to complete. |
| Instructions | Clear, directive | Enter your Social Security number. Don't include dashes. |
| Errors | Helpful, solution-focused | We need your email address to send you updates. Enter a valid email. |
| Success | Positive, affirming | You submitted your application! We'll review it and contact you within 10 business days. |
| Sensitive topics | Empathetic, respectful | We know this information is personal. We're required by federal law to ask, and we always keep your data secure. |


## Empowerment language

Language shapes how people feel about their own agency. In government content especially, the difference between "you can" and "you may be eligible to" isn't just stylistic; it's the difference between feeling welcome and feeling screened out.

- "Your practice" and "your business" — not "the business"
- "Get paid" — not "receive compensation"
- "You can" — not "you may be eligible to"
- "You need to" — not "it is required that you"
- "We'll review your application" — not "your application will be reviewed"
