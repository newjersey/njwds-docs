How content is visually structured and organized on the page to help users scan and navigate content.

## Bold text

Use bold sparingly and purposefully — only to highlight genuinely critical information a user might otherwise miss. Overusing bold dilutes its effect; if everything is emphasized, nothing is.
- Bold a key term on first use, a critical deadline, or a required action
- Don't bold for decoration 
- Don't bold as a substitute for a heading
- Don't bold entire sentences or paragraphs

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| We’re excited to share our new product line. | We’re **excited** to share our **new** product line. |
| All changes must be submitted through the official portal and cannot be emailed directly. | **All changes must be submitted through the official portal and cannot be emailed directly.** |
| The deadline to reapply is **March 15**.  | The deadline to reapply is March 15.  |


## Bulleted and numbered lists

### General guidance
- Use numbered lists for steps that must be followed in a specific order
- Use bulleted lists for any list that doesn't require a specific order
- Do not use a list for only one item
- Follow the correct punctuation rules for lists with full or incomplete sentences

### Introducing a list

**Do not split the introduction across the sentence and the list items.**
This happens when the introductory sentence is incomplete on its own and only makes sense when read together with the list items. This creates problems for translation, screen readers, and users who scan rather than read linearly.

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| ... | ... |

In the "don’t" version, none of the bullet items make sense without the introductory sentence. A screen reader user navigating directly to the list, or a translator working item by item, would lose the meaning entirely.

**Introduce every list with a full sentence.** 
A label or heading is not a list introduction. A full sentence sets context and tells the user what they're about to read and why it matters.

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| You'll need 3 documents to apply: | Documents: |
| There are 3 ways to contact us: | Ways to contact us: |

**Avoid "the following" and use a more direct phrase instead.** 
"The following" is a filler phrase that adds words without adding meaning. It also sounds formal and institutional.

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| You'll need 3 documents to apply: | You’ll need the following documents: |
| Your application must include: | Your application must include the following: |
| ... | ... |

### Length
- Keep each list item to no more than 1–2 sentences
- If a list has more than seven items, break it into smaller lists with descriptive subheadings

### Conjunctions in lists (and/or)
- Always separate "and" lists from "or" lists. Never mix them in a single list
- Do not use "and/or.”  Restructure to list all options: "your income, your net worth, or both”
- For complex eligibility criteria, use "and" or "or" at the end of each item to make the relationship between requirements clear. Use a comma before the conjunction and bold the "and" or "or"

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| You have a current illness or injury that affects your mind or body, and.. | You have a current illness that affects your mind and/or body |
| ... | ... |

### Nesting
Use only one level of bullet points. Avoid sub-bullets — they make content harder to scan. If you need sub-bullets, consider making the parent item a separate list with its own subheading.


### Ordering lists
Always organize lists in a way that helps users understand or choose.
- Location: geographic or physical order (for lists of places or facilities)
- Grouped by type: keep similar items together
- Popularity or commonality: most common option first (use data, not assumption)
- Importance or urgency: most important or time-sensitive item first
- Numerical or chronological: low to high, or earliest to latest
- Alphabetical: for items like medications or locations with no other natural order; don't default to alphabetical for all lists

## Descriptive linking

### General guidance
Link text should tell users exactly where they're going or what they'll find, before they click on it. This matters for all users, but especially for people using screen readers, which often navigate by pulling up a list of links on a page. If every link says "learn more" or "click here," that list is useless. 

Never use:
- "Click here"
- "Learn more"
- "Read more"
- "Here"
- "This page"
- "More information"

Instead, describe the destination or action:
- "Learn how to apply for NJ FamilyCare"
- "Download the WorkFirst NJ application"
- "Find a licensed contractor in New Jersey"
- "Renew your professional license"

#### Additional resources:
- [NJIA Accessibility Enablement Guide](https://github.com/newjersey/enablement/blob/main/accessibility/playbooks/checklist-for-teams.md#check-6-links-and-buttons)
- Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG): [Guide on link purpose (in context)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/link-purpose-in-context) and [descriptive linking](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G53)
- Section508.gov: [Descriptive links and hypertext](https://www.section508.gov/blog/accessibility-bytes/descriptive-links-and-hypertext/)
- Federal Plain Language Guidelines: [Links best practices](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/design/links)

**Keep link text concise but complete.**
Link the phrase that describes what the user will get.  Don't link an entire sentence or a single word.

| ✅ Do | ❌  Don’t |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| Find out if you [qualify for NJ family care](https://njfamilycare.dhs.state.nj.us/). | [Find out](https://njfamilycare.dhs.state.nj.us/) if you qualify for NJ family care. |
|   | [Find out if you qualify for NJ FamilyCare by visiting our eligibility page and reviewing the income and residency requirements listed there](https://njfamilycare.dhs.state.nj.us/). |

**Don't repeat link text that goes to different destinations.** 
If two links on the same page both say "learn more," users — especially screen reader users — have no way to distinguish them.

**For documents and downloads, include the file type**
- Download the 2025 benefit guide (PDF)
- Open the renewal checklist (PDF)

### Links in lists
List items can be links, but avoid overwhelming users with a long list of links. If one link applies to all items in the list, add it on a separate line after the list

If every item is a link, keep the list to fewer than seven items, and don't link to the same page twice

#### Option 1: Link on a separate line below the item:
Use this option when the list item stands on its own as useful information, but has one specific related action or resource that some users need. This approach works well when:
- You need to mix linked and unlinked items in the same list
- The link is supplementary rather than the primary next step
- You want users to be able to read the list without feeling like every item demands an action

##### Example:
Burial in a VA national cemetery includes these benefits:
- A gravesite in any national cemetery with available space <br> [Find a national cemetery near you](https://www.cem.va.gov/find-cemetery/all-national.asp)
- A headstone or marker provided by the government <br> [Learn about headstone eligibility](https://www.cem.va.gov/hmm/)
- Perpetual care of the gravesite

#### Option 2: For lists of brief items, link each list item, but keep the list short
Use this option when the list items are brief and every item leads somewhere different. This approach works well when:
- All items are very short noun phrases or program names
- There are multiple links to different pages
- You don’t need users to take a specific action

##### Example:
We offer these health and wellness programs:
- [Nutrition and food assistance](https://www.nutrition.gov/topics/food-security-and-access/food-assistance-programs)
- [Tobacco cessation support](https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/hcp/patient-care/quitlines-and-other-resources.html)
- [Weight management programs](https://www.nj.gov/health/nutrition/services-support/index.shtml)
- [Women's health services](https://www.nj.gov/health/womens-health/)

#### Option 3: Link at the end as a separate sentence
Adding the link as its own sentence keeps the descriptive content and the call to action clearly separated. It also forces you to write more specific, action-oriented link text because the link can't lean on the surrounding sentence for context. Use this when:
- The item needs a sentence or two of explanation before the link makes sense
- You want to clearly separate the descriptive content from the call to action
- You need space to write specific, action-oriented link text

##### Example:
You may be eligible for these NJ benefits:
- **NJ FamilyCare.** Free or low-cost health coverage for families and individuals who meet income requirements. [Find out if you qualify for NJ FamilyCare](https://njfamilycare.dhs.state.nj.us/)
- **WorkFirst NJ.** Temporary financial assistance while you look for work. Learn how to apply for [WorkFirst NJ](https://www.nj.gov/humanservices/wfnj/)


#### Option 4: Link goes after the list
Use this option when the list item has enough context or detail that making the entire item a link would be overwhelming, but the link is still essential. This approach works well when:
- The list is a set of requirements that apply to a single process
- One link applies to all the content in the bulleted list, not just 1 or 2 bullets
- You want to keep the descriptive content and the call to action clearly separated

##### Example:
To renew your license you will need:
- Your current license number
- Proof of completed continuing education hours
- Payment for the renewal fee

[Renew your professional license.](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/renewals)

## Headings and page titles
Headings are navigation tools, not labels. A user scanning a page should be able to read only the headings and understand what the page covers and where to find what they need. 

### Headings:
- Use headings in logical order (H1 → H2 → H3). Do not skip levels; [this is required for accessibility](https://github.com/newjersey/enablement/blob/main/accessibility/playbooks/checklist-for-teams.md#check-4-headings-and-labels)
- Write headings that are clear, specific, and descriptive
- Front-load headings with the most meaningful word
  - ✓ Correct: "Documents you need to apply"
  - ✗ Incorrect: "What you will need to have ready before beginning your application"
- Use sentence case (visit the Capitalization section for more guidance)
- Do not link text within headings
- Do not use ending punctuation in headings, unless the header is a question
- Consider writing headings as questions when users are likely scanning for a specific answer
  - ✓ Correct: "How long does it take to process my application?"
- Use parallel construction across headings at the same level (all start with nouns, or all start with verbs)
- Use subheadings to break up long sections, but make sure each subheading earns its place. If a section only has one paragraph, it probably doesn't need a subheading


### Page titles: 
- Keep page titles to 65 characters or fewer (including spaces)
- [Use page titles that describe the current page, website, or service](https://github.com/newjersey/enablement/blob/main/accessibility/playbooks/checklist-for-teams.md#check-5-page-title)


## Tables
Use tables to present comparisons, options, or structured data — they're often easier to scan than paragraphs or long lists.

Tables work well for:
- Comparing eligibility criteria across different groups
- Showing fees, deadlines, or requirements side by side
- Presenting options with multiple attributes

Tables do not work well for:
- Simple lists that don't need comparison
- Information that reads naturally as prose
- Content that will be accessed primarily on mobile (tables are hard to read on small screens — consider an alternative format)

Always include clear, descriptive column headers. Don't use a table just because you have data, use it when the tabular format genuinely makes the relationship between items clearer.
