## Core principles
These principles define our relationship with the people we serve. They guide every decision we make: from how we structure information, to the content strategies we implement.

1. **Empower.** We help people understand what they need to do, why it matters, and that they're capable of doing it. We design without assuming prior experience or system knowledge, and we don't make people jump through hoops to find what they need.
2. **Respect.** We put ourselves in the reader's position. They might have limited time, resources, low literacy, language barriers, real stakes, and often significant stress. We never patronize our users or take their time for granted. 
3. **Educate.** We give people the facts they need to take action, not just what is easy to explain. We practice progressive disclosure: breaking down complex policy into more digestible information and providing critical details when a user needs to make a decision. By offering clear paths to deeper context, we ensure relevant information is never buried in a wall of text. 
4. **Guide.** We act as a knowledgeable neighbor, not a gatekeeper. Whether someone is completing a form or looking up a benefit, we walk them through it step by step in a way that feels manageable, not bureaucratic.
5. **Be authoritative.** We are the source of truth and state facts clearly and directly. We avoid hedging language like "hopefully" or "we aim to." If a rule exists, say so. If a timeline is fixed, give it. People follow direct and clear guidance.


### Writing goals
These are the standards we hold every piece of writing to. They're practical checks to make sure our principles show up in our work. 

- **Clear.** Know your topic well enough to explain it simply. Use plain words and short sentences. If something is hard to write plainly, it usually needs more thought, not more words.
- **Useful.** Every sentence should answer a question, prompt an action, or reduce uncertainty. Before you write, ask: what does this person need right now? What will they do with this information? 
- **Human.** Write like a person, not an institution. Don’t be afraid to break grammar rules if it makes your writing feel more relatable and conversational. Use contractions, start sentences with "And" or "But," and prioritize how people actually speak over ancient textbook rules. 
- **Appropriate.** Match your tone to the moment. Instructions call for directness. Sensitive topics call for empathy. A success message can be warm. Adapt your tone without losing your voice.
- **Inclusive.** New Jersey's residents speak many languages, come from many backgrounds, and have varying levels of digital experience. Write for all of them — not just the easiest reader to imagine.
- **Trustworthy.** Accuracy builds trust more than tone does. Use real timelines, cite real processes, and never overstate what we can deliver.
